# 1. Rooftop Solar for Homes & Businesses

## Turn Your Rooftop into a Source of Clean Electricity

### Overview

Your rooftop can be more than a protective structure—it can become a productive source of electricity.

Rooftop solar systems use photovoltaic (PV) panels to convert sunlight into electrical energy. The electricity can power household appliances, lighting, air conditioning, office equipment, computers, and other connected loads.

For grid-connected installations, solar electricity can be used directly by the property, while additional electricity may be exported to the utility grid where permitted. When solar production is insufficient, electricity can be drawn from the grid.

System design depends on electricity consumption, roof area, orientation, shading, structural suitability, and local grid requirements.

AeroSun Energy can help customers evaluate rooftop solar options based on their property and energy needs.

### How Rooftop Solar Works

**1. Solar panels capture sunlight**

Photovoltaic cells convert sunlight into direct-current (DC) electricity.

**2. The inverter converts electricity**

A suitable inverter converts DC electricity into alternating-current (AC) electricity used by most household and commercial equipment.

**3. Solar electricity powers connected loads**

Electricity generated during daylight hours can supply appliances, office equipment, lighting, and other loads operating at the time.

**4. The grid supplies additional electricity**

When consumption exceeds solar production, the property can draw the difference from the utility grid, subject to the system configuration.

**5. Surplus electricity may be exported**

Where permitted by the applicable utility arrangements, excess generation may be exported to the grid. Export compensation and settlement depend on the relevant regulations and tariff.

### Applications

**Residential properties**
- Independent houses and villas
- Apartments and residential buildings, subject to suitable ownership and metering arrangements
- Lighting and household appliances
- Refrigerators and washing machines
- Computers and home-office equipment
- Air conditioning and other daytime loads

**Commercial properties**
- Shops and retail outlets
- Offices and professional establishments
- Hotels and restaurants
- Clinics and small healthcare facilities
- Educational institutions
- Warehouses and service centres

### Key Benefits

**Potential electricity bill reduction**

Solar electricity consumed on site can reduce the amount of electricity purchased from the utility.

**Better use of roof space**

Suitable rooftops can generate electricity without requiring a separate ground-mounted installation area.

**Reduced dependence on grid electricity**

Solar can supply part of a property's electricity demand during daylight hours.

**Long-term energy planning**

A properly designed system can provide a useful basis for forecasting solar generation and expected electricity cost offsets.

**Scalable capacity**

System capacity can be selected according to available roof space, electricity consumption, and electrical infrastructure.

**Environmental benefits**

Solar generation produces electricity without direct combustion emissions during operation.

### Example Calculation: Residential Rooftop Solar

Consider a home installing a **5 kW rooftop solar system**.

Assume:
- Solar capacity: 5 kW
- Average equivalent full-sun hours: 5 hours/day
- Performance ratio: 80%
- Electricity tariff: ₹8/kWh

The full-sun-hours assumption is illustrative. The actual value varies by location, season, orientation, and weather.

**Step 1: Estimate daily solar generation**

Daily generation = Solar capacity × Equivalent full-sun hours × Performance ratio

5 × 5 × 0.80 = **20 kWh/day**

**Step 2: Estimate annual generation**

20 × 365 = **7,300 kWh/year**

**Step 3: Estimate the gross value of generated electricity**

7,300 × ₹8 = **₹58,400/year**

This represents an illustrative gross electricity value, not guaranteed bill savings.

If the household directly consumes 70% of its solar generation:

7,300 × 70% = **5,110 kWh/year consumed on site**

The approximate value of this self-consumed electricity is:

5,110 × ₹8 = **₹40,880/year**

The remaining 30% may be exported, stored, or curtailed depending on the system configuration and applicable arrangements. Its financial value must be calculated separately.

# 2. Off-grid Solar for Remote Power

## Reliable Solar Electricity Where Grid Access Is Limited

### Overview

Remote homes, agricultural sites, field stations, telecommunications facilities, and isolated infrastructure may have limited or unreliable access to the utility grid.

Off-grid solar systems generate electricity locally and use battery storage to supply loads when sunlight is unavailable or insufficient.

Unlike a standard grid-connected solar installation, an off-grid system must be designed to meet the required loads without depending on a continuous utility connection. This requires careful planning of solar capacity, battery energy, inverter power, operating schedules, and backup provisions.

An off-grid system can also include a generator or another suitable energy source where additional reliability is required.

### How an Off-grid Solar System Works

**1. Solar generation**

Solar panels produce electricity during daylight hours.

**2. Charge control**

Suitable power electronics regulate energy from the solar array and manage battery charging.

**3. Battery storage**

The battery stores surplus solar electricity for evening and night-time use.

**4. Inverter operation**

An off-grid inverter supplies the required AC electricity to connected equipment.

**5. Load management**

Essential loads can be prioritized during low-generation periods to preserve battery energy.

**6. Optional backup generation**

Where continuous availability is essential, a backup generator or another dependable source can support the system during extended periods of poor solar generation.

### Applications

- Remote houses and farmhouses
- Rural clinics and health centres
- Telecommunications and monitoring stations
- Forest and environmental monitoring equipment
- Remote warehouses and work sites
- Field research facilities
- Off-grid agricultural buildings
- Rural lighting systems
- Remote water supply installations
- Isolated infrastructure facilities

### Key Benefits

**Energy access without a continuous grid connection**

Off-grid solar can supply electricity where utility service is unavailable or impractical.

**Reduced dependence on fuel**

Solar generation can reduce generator operating hours and associated fuel consumption.

**Energy storage for night-time use**

Batteries can provide electricity after sunset, subject to available stored energy and load requirements.

**Modular system design**

Generation and storage can be selected according to daily energy consumption and required autonomy.

**Remote monitoring opportunities**

Suitable monitoring equipment can help operators review system condition and identify faults.

**Potential reduction in operating costs**

Savings depend on the previous energy source, system cost, battery replacement, maintenance, and local operating conditions.

### Example Calculation: Off-grid Remote House

Consider a remote house with the following estimated daily loads:

| Electrical load | Power | Usage per day | Energy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LED lighting | 100 W | 5 hours | 0.50 kWh |
| Fans | 200 W total | 8 hours | 1.60 kWh |
| Refrigerator | 150 W average while running | 8 equivalent running hours | 1.20 kWh |
| Television and electronics | 100 W | 4 hours | 0.40 kWh |
| Water pump | 750 W | 1 hour | 0.75 kWh |
| Other small loads | — | — | 0.55 kWh |
| **Total** | | | **5.00 kWh/day** |

The refrigerator's energy use is represented by equivalent running hours; its actual consumption should be measured or estimated from its energy rating and operating conditions.

**Step 1: Calculate daily energy demand**

Total estimated consumption = **5 kWh/day**

**Step 2: Estimate solar panel capacity**

Assume:
- Equivalent full-sun hours: 5 hours/day
- Solar performance ratio: 75%

Required solar capacity = Daily demand ÷ (Full-sun hours × Performance ratio)

5 ÷ (5 × 0.75) = **1.33 kW**

A preliminary design might consider approximately **1.5–2 kW of solar PV**, depending on seasonal conditions, battery charging needs, and future loads.

This calculation is a starting estimate, not a final system specification.

**Step 3: Estimate battery capacity for one day of autonomy**

Daily energy demand = 5 kWh

Assume:
- Battery usable fraction: 80%
- Inverter discharge efficiency: 90%

Nominal battery capacity = Daily AC energy demand ÷ (Usable fraction × Inverter efficiency)

5 ÷ (0.80 × 0.90) = **6.94 kWh**

A preliminary battery selection might therefore be around **7–8 kWh nominal**, subject to the battery's permitted operating limits and the required reserve.

**Step 4: Consider two days of autonomy**

Required AC energy:

5 × 2 = 10 kWh

Nominal battery capacity:

10 ÷ (0.80 × 0.90) = **13.89 kWh**

A battery of approximately **14–16 kWh nominal** may be a starting point for further design, but the final selection must consider the load profile, battery power, temperature, ageing, and low-sun conditions.

- Solar availability during the least favourable season.

# 3. Hybrid Solar with Backup

## Solar Power with Storage for Greater Energy Flexibility

### Overview

Solar power can reduce electricity purchases during daylight hours, but solar generation decreases in the evening and may be limited during cloudy weather.

A hybrid solar system combines solar panels, a compatible inverter, battery storage, and a grid connection or backup generator where appropriate.

Depending on the equipment, the system can use solar electricity for active loads, charge the battery with surplus energy, and supply selected loads from stored energy when solar generation is insufficient.

A system designed for backup operation can also supply designated loads during a grid outage. However, not every grid-connected solar installation provides backup power. The inverter, battery, transfer arrangements, and electrical protection must be specifically designed for this function.

### How Hybrid Solar with Backup Works

**1. Solar panels generate electricity**

During daylight hours, solar generation supplies the property's electrical demand.

**2. Surplus electricity charges the battery**

When generation exceeds the energy required by active loads, the system can charge the battery according to its operating settings.

**3. The battery supplies selected loads**

When solar generation falls, the battery can provide electricity to designated loads, subject to its remaining energy and output limits.

**4. Grid electricity supplements the system**

If the battery is depleted or the connected load exceeds available capacity, the grid can supply additional electricity where connected.

**5. Backup operation supports selected loads**

During a grid outage, a suitably configured hybrid inverter may isolate the backed-up circuits and continue supplying them using solar generation and battery energy.

**6. Energy management protects battery reserves**

Operating settings can prioritize essential loads and preserve a reserve for outages, depending on the system design.

### Applications

- Homes requiring selected-load backup
- Offices and small commercial establishments
- Clinics and medical offices
- Shops and retail outlets
- Small workshops
- Rural properties with unreliable grid supply
- Facilities with critical lighting, communications, or control equipment
- Properties seeking a combination of solar savings and backup power

### Key Benefits

**Daytime electricity cost reduction**

Solar energy can offset part of the electricity that would otherwise be purchased from the grid.

**Stored energy for later use**

A battery can shift some solar energy from daylight hours to evening or night-time use.

**Backup for designated circuits**

A suitably configured system can maintain power to selected loads during grid outages.

**Greater control over energy use**

Operating settings can prioritize self-consumption, battery reserve, or other customer requirements.

**Flexible system configuration**

Solar capacity, battery size, and backed-up loads can be selected according to the intended use.

**Reduced generator dependence**

Where a generator is currently used for backup, solar and batteries may reduce its operating hours, depending on the system design and weather.

### Example Calculation: Hybrid Solar for a Home

Consider a home with:
- Solar PV: 5 kW
- Equivalent full-sun hours: 5 hours/day
- Solar performance ratio: 80%
- Battery: 5 kWh nominal
- Battery usable fraction: 90%
- Battery discharge efficiency: 92%

**Step 1: Estimate daily solar generation**

5 × 5 × 0.80 = **20 kWh/day**

**Step 2: Estimate usable battery energy**

5 × 0.90 × 0.92 = **4.14 kWh**

**Step 3: Estimate backup duration**

Suppose the backed-up loads consume 500 W, or 0.5 kW, continuously.

Backup duration = Usable AC battery energy ÷ Load

4.14 ÷ 0.5 = **approximately 8.3 hours**

This estimate assumes the battery starts fully charged and does not account for additional standby loads, battery ageing, temperature, or reserve settings.

If the backed-up load increases to 1 kW:

4.14 ÷ 1 = **approximately 4.1 hours**

This illustrates why backup duration depends on both battery capacity and the power consumed by the backed-up loads.

### Example: Estimate the Value of Solar Electricity

If the 5 kW system generates 20 kWh/day and the household consumes 70% directly:

20 × 70% = **14 kWh/day consumed on site**

At an illustrative tariff of ₹8/kWh:

14 × ₹8 = **₹112/day**

Annual gross value, assuming the same average daily generation and consumption pattern throughout the year:

₹112 × 365 = **₹40,880/year**

This is an illustrative value of self-consumed solar electricity. It is not guaranteed savings or profit. Actual savings depend on seasonal generation, the household's load profile, applicable tariffs, export compensation, battery losses, and system costs.

### Important Design Considerations

- Which loads need backup?
- How long must backup power last?
- What is the highest simultaneous load?
- Are there motors, pumps, or air conditioners with high starting currents?
- How much battery reserve should be retained?
- Is the system designed to supply loads during a grid outage?
- Can the solar array charge the battery while supporting active loads?
- Is a generator required for extended outages?

# 4. Solar Pumpsets & Agriculture

## Solar-Powered Water Pumping for Farms and Agricultural Operations

### Overview

Water availability and pumping costs are important factors in agricultural productivity. Solar pumpsets use electricity generated by photovoltaic panels to operate water pumps for irrigation and other agricultural water requirements.

A solar pumping system can be designed for borewells, open wells, reservoirs, tanks, drip irrigation, or sprinkler irrigation, depending on the water source and hydraulic requirements.

The correct system depends on more than the pump's motor rating. Water flow, total pumping head, pipe friction, seasonal water availability, irrigation schedules, and the required operating hours must all be considered.

For some applications, solar pumping can operate directly during daylight hours. Other installations may use storage tanks, batteries, grid power, or a hybrid arrangement to meet operational requirements.

### How Solar Pumping Works

**1. Solar panels generate electricity**

The PV array produces electricity from sunlight.

**2. A suitable controller manages pump operation**

A solar pump controller or compatible variable-frequency drive manages the electrical supply to the pump according to the system design.

**3. The pump moves water**

The pump transfers water from the source to an irrigation network, storage tank, or reservoir.

**4. Water storage supports flexible irrigation**

Water can be pumped when solar generation is available and stored for later use, reducing the need for electrical battery storage in suitable applications.

**5. Sensors help protect the system**

Water-level sensors and suitable controls can help prevent dry running, monitor tank levels, and manage operating schedules.

**6. Optional hybrid operation provides flexibility**

Grid power, battery storage, or backup generation may be considered when pumping must continue outside solar production hours.

### Applications

- Borewell irrigation
- Open-well pumping
- Drip irrigation
- Sprinkler irrigation
- Orchard and plantation irrigation
- Greenhouses
- Livestock water supply
- Farm reservoirs and storage tanks
- Water transfer between fields
- Rural agricultural water systems

### Key Benefits

**Reduced reliance on grid electricity or diesel**

Solar pumping can reduce the need for purchased electricity or fuel, depending on the existing pumping arrangement.

**Daytime water pumping**

Solar power can be used when sunlight is available, allowing pumping schedules to be aligned with generation.

**Potentially lower operating expenditure**

Fuel costs and generator maintenance may decrease where solar replaces diesel pumping. Financial results depend on system cost and actual operation.

**Flexible irrigation planning**

Water storage and pump scheduling can help coordinate energy availability with irrigation needs.

**Remote agricultural suitability**

Solar pumping may be practical where grid connections are weak, unreliable, or unavailable.

**Monitoring and protection options**

Appropriate controllers and sensors can help manage pump operation and protect equipment.

### Example Calculation: Solar Pump for Irrigation

Consider a farm with:
- Pump motor input: 5.5 kW
- Pumping duration: 5 hours/day
- Operating days: 200 days/year
- Illustrative grid electricity tariff: ₹6/kWh

**Step 1: Calculate daily pumping energy**

5.5 × 5 = **27.5 kWh/day**

**Step 2: Calculate annual pumping energy**

27.5 × 200 = **5,500 kWh/year**

**Step 3: Estimate the gross value of displaced electricity**

5,500 × ₹6 = **₹33,000/year**

If solar pumping replaces 5,500 kWh of grid electricity that would otherwise have been purchased at ₹6/kWh, the gross electricity value is ₹33,000 per year.

This is not a guaranteed net saving. Actual financial benefit depends on solar system cost, maintenance, water demand, operating schedules, existing power supply, and any subsidies or incentives for which the customer qualifies.

### Example Calculation: Preliminary Solar Array Sizing

Suppose the pump requires 27.5 kWh/day.

Assume:
- Equivalent full-sun hours: 5 hours/day
- Overall solar-to-useful-energy performance factor: 75%

Preliminary PV capacity:

27.5 ÷ (5 × 0.75) = **7.33 kW**

This suggests a preliminary array in the region of 7.5 kW or higher for further engineering assessment.

It is not a final pump-system specification. Pump starting requirements, controller limits, changing sunlight, hydraulic performance, seasonal water demand, and required pumping hours must be assessed before selecting equipment.

### Why Water Storage Can Be Valuable

Consider a farm that needs to irrigate crops in the evening but has good solar availability during the day.

Instead of storing electricity in a large battery to operate the pump later, the system may pump water into a tank or reservoir during daylight hours.

The stored water can then be used when required, subject to adequate tank capacity, water pressure, and irrigation design.

This approach can reduce battery requirements in suitable applications.

- Daily and seasonal irrigation demand.
- Pump motor and controller compatibility.
- Solar panel capacity and mounting structure.
- Pipe sizing, valves, and water storage.
- Dry-run protection and tank-level control.

# 6. Solar Battery Storage & Energy Independence

## Store Solar Electricity for Greater Control Over Energy Use

### Overview

Solar panels generate electricity when sunlight is available, but many properties also require electricity in the evening, at night, or during grid interruptions.

Battery energy storage can retain part of the solar electricity generated during the day and make it available later.

A solar-plus-battery system may be designed to improve self-consumption, support selected loads during outages, or provide off-grid electricity when combined with adequate generation and suitable power-conversion equipment.

Battery storage is not automatically necessary for every solar installation. Its value depends on electricity tariffs, load timing, backup requirements, battery cost, and the way the system will be operated.

### How Solar Battery Storage Works

**1. Solar panels generate electricity**

During daylight hours, solar generation supplies active loads.

**2. Surplus electricity charges the battery**

When generation exceeds immediate demand, the system may direct the surplus into the battery.

**3. Stored electricity is used later**

The battery discharges when configured to support evening consumption, selected loads, or another defined operating priority.

**4. The controller manages energy flows**

The energy management system monitors battery state of charge, power demand, solar output, and grid availability.

**5. Backup operation supports selected circuits**

A compatible inverter and appropriate electrical arrangements can supply designated circuits during a grid outage.

**6. Grid or backup power fills remaining gaps**

When solar and stored energy are insufficient, the system may draw from the grid or another available source.

### Applications

- Homes seeking evening solar utilization
- Offices with critical IT and communication equipment
- Shops and small businesses
- Clinics and healthcare facilities
- Remote sites
- Small industrial facilities
- Rural homes and farm buildings
- Properties with unreliable grid supply
- Hybrid solar and wind installations

### Key Benefits

**Use solar electricity beyond daylight hours**

A battery can shift some electricity generated during the day to later periods.

**Potential increase in solar self-consumption**

Storage may allow surplus solar electricity to be used later instead of being exported, where the system and tariff arrangements make that beneficial.

**Backup for selected loads**

A suitable system can provide electricity during grid interruptions.

**Greater control over energy scheduling**

The system can be configured to preserve battery reserves or follow defined charge and discharge priorities.

**Support for off-grid systems**

Batteries are an important component of many off-grid systems, although the generation and storage must be sized together.

### Example Calculation: Battery Backup

Suppose a home has the following backed-up loads:

| Load | Power | Operating time | Daily energy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Lighting | 100 W | 5 hours | 0.50 kWh |
| Fans | 150 W | 6 hours | 0.90 kWh |
| Router and communications | 20 W | 10 hours | 0.20 kWh |
| Laptop and small electronics | 100 W | 3 hours | 0.30 kWh |
| **Total** | | | **1.90 kWh** |

Assume the homeowner wants to support these loads for one day.

**Step 1: Calculate required AC energy**

Total = **1.90 kWh**

**Step 2: Calculate nominal battery capacity**

Assume:
- Usable battery fraction: 90%
- Discharge conversion efficiency: 92%

Nominal capacity:

1.90 ÷ (0.90 × 0.92) = **2.29 kWh**

A nominal battery around 2.5–3 kWh may be a starting point for evaluation, subject to the manufacturer's specifications and the required reserve.

**Step 3: Check the power requirement**

The total listed power, if all loads operate simultaneously, is:

100 + 150 + 20 + 100 = **370 W**

The inverter must support the actual simultaneous load and any surge requirements. The battery and inverter should also be compatible in terms of voltage, current, and power limits.

This example shows why battery selection must consider both **energy capacity (kWh)** and **power capability (kW)**.

### Important Financial Consideration

A battery may improve backup capability and solar self-consumption, but it also adds capital cost and energy losses.

Before selecting storage, compare:
- The value of electricity that can be shifted.
- The value of backup power for the intended loads.
- Battery cost and expected service life.
- Round-trip efficiency and operating limits.
- Maintenance and replacement costs.
- The value of grid exports, where permitted.
- The cost and availability of alternative backup sources.

A household, an industrial facility, and an irrigation pump do not have the same energy profile. System capacity should reflect the intended application rather than relying on a standard size for every customer.

### Practical System Sizing

Solar panels, inverters, batteries, and pumps must be selected as compatible components. Generation estimates should account for sunlight, shading, orientation, temperature, conversion losses, and seasonal changes.

### Appropriate Technology Selection

Grid-connected, off-grid, hybrid, and solar pumping systems serve different purposes. Selecting the appropriate configuration helps avoid unnecessary equipment and unsuitable operating expectations.

### Transparent Generation Estimates

Generation estimates should state the assumptions used and distinguish annual average generation from actual output on individual days.

### Safety and Long-term Operation

Electrical protection, grounding, mounting structures, maintenance access, and equipment compatibility are important parts of a well-designed installation.

### Expandability Where Practical

Future energy requirements can be considered during the initial design, subject to available space, electrical infrastructure, and system compatibility.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## 1. How much electricity can a solar panel system generate?

Generation depends on system capacity, local sunlight, shading, orientation, temperature, equipment efficiency, and weather. A preliminary estimate can be calculated using equivalent full-sun hours and an appropriate performance ratio, but a site-specific assessment provides a better estimate.

## 2. Can rooftop solar eliminate my electricity bill?

Not necessarily. Solar can reduce electricity purchases, but the remaining bill may include electricity imported at night, fixed charges, demand charges, taxes, and other applicable fees. Export compensation also varies by the applicable arrangement.

## 3. What is the difference between grid-connected and off-grid solar?

A grid-connected system operates alongside the utility grid and generally relies on it when solar generation is insufficient. An off-grid system must supply its loads independently and typically requires battery storage or another suitable source of dispatchable electricity.

## 4. Does rooftop solar provide power during a grid outage?

A standard grid-connected inverter generally shuts down during a grid outage for safety. Backup operation requires compatible equipment and an appropriate system design.

## 5. Is a battery required for rooftop solar?

No. Many grid-connected rooftop systems operate without batteries. Batteries are useful where storage, selected-load backup, or off-grid operation is required.

## 6. Can solar power run an irrigation pump?

Yes, if the solar array, pump, controller, and hydraulic system are correctly sized. The design must account for flow, total dynamic head, operating hours, and seasonal water requirements.

## 7. How much roof space is required?

Roof area depends on the required system capacity, panel dimensions, layout, orientation, access paths, shading, and setbacks. A site survey and layout plan are needed to establish the usable area.

## 8. How long does a solar system last?

Service life varies by component and operating conditions. Solar panels, inverters, batteries, mounting structures, and protection equipment have different expected service lives and warranty conditions. Equipment warranties should not be treated as guarantees of overall system performance.

## 9. Can solar and wind power be combined?

Yes. Solar and wind can be integrated in a hybrid system where the site conditions and project economics justify both technologies. Wind generation should be based on an appropriate resource assessment rather than assumed from the presence of occasional wind.

## 10. What information is needed for a solar assessment?

Useful starting information includes the site location, recent electricity bills, connected loads, operating hours, available roof or ground area, grid connection details, and any backup requirements.
